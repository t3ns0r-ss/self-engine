## Goal

Phase 5 teaches you to answer many questions about ranges of an array without looking at every element, and to choose the structure that fits the question. The array may never change, or single values may change, or whole ranges may change; the answer may be a minimum, a sum, a count of pairs, or a number that no formula over halves gives. Each of these asks for a different tool. The four topics go from a table that answers in a single step, to a tree that handles sums and counts with changes, to a tree that handles every kind of range operation, and then to the methods that take over when no tree fits: blocks of about $\sqrt n$ positions, a clever order for offline questions, and a threshold that splits large cases from small ones. Each lesson proves when its method is correct and lists the conditions that break it. By the end you should solve AtCoder Beginner Contest (ABC) E–G range problems routinely and Codeforces problems rated about **1800–2100**, the target band of this phase.

## Topics

Work through them in order; each lesson lists what it uses from the earlier ones.

| topic | what it gives you |
|---|---|
| [5.1 Sparse table](5-1-sparse-table/) | Range minimum, maximum, GCD, AND and OR on an array that never changes, answered in one step after one build; searching along a growing range; counting over all starts when the aggregate changes only a few times. |
| [5.2 Fenwick tree](5-2-fenwick/) | Single changes with range sums, additions to a range with single reads, counting inversions, answering range questions offline in the order of their right ends, and finding the $k$-th element of a changing collection. |
| [5.3 Segment tree](5-3-segment-tree/) | Changes with range minimums, maximums and GCDs, lazy tags for range additions and assignments, nodes with several numbers, and finding the first position that fits by walking down the tree. |
| [5.4 Square-root decomposition and Mo's algorithm](5-4-sqrt-decomposition/) | Blocks of about $\sqrt n$ positions with a note or a sorted copy, a good order for offline range questions, and a threshold that splits large cases from small ones. |

## Time

Plan on **15 to 20 hours per topic**: about 3 hours to read the lesson and redo its proofs and traces, 10 to 15 hours for the ladder of 12–20 problems, and 2 hours for the drill and the checkpoint. With the reviews spread over the following two months, Phase 5 takes roughly **70 to 90 hours**. Keep taking part in one live contest a week; in a typical contest a problem about many questions on an array is a range problem, and the work is to notice what kind of range problem.

## The Phase 5 exam

After the four checkpoints, take the [Phase 5 exam](exam/): 6 problems in **4 hours**, in a mixed order with the topics hidden. You pass by solving at least 60% of the problems in time (4 of 6) with no Recognition miss on a solved problem, that is, without first trying a wrong tool on a problem you then solved. After the exam, a remediation map shows for each problem the topic, the card, and the lesson section to reread.
