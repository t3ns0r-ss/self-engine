## Goal

Phase 1 turns the brute-force habits of Phase 0 into the standard techniques for arrays: sort first and look at neighbours, answer range questions from prefix values, move two pointers instead of trying every pair, search on the answer, prove a greedy rule before trusting it, find the nearest larger element with a stack, and reason about numbers one bit at a time. Each technique replaces an $O(n^2)$ idea with an $O(n)$ or $O(n \log n)$ one, and each comes with the property that makes it correct. By the end you should solve AtCoder Beginner Contest (ABC) problems C–D reliably and many E problems, and Codeforces problems rated about **1000–1400**, the target band of this phase.

## Topics

Work through them in order; each lesson lists what it uses from the earlier ones.

| topic | what it gives you |
|---|---|
| [1.1 Sorting](1-1-sorting/) | Sorting then scanning, comparators and their rules, sorting with positions, coordinate compression, sweeping sorted events. |
| [1.2 Prefix sums and difference arrays](1-2-prefix-sums/) | Range sums in $O(1)$, counting subarrays by pairs of prefix values, many range updates, 2D rectangles, best values before or after a position. |
| [1.3 Two pointers and sliding window](1-3-two-pointers/) | Windows that shrink on failure, counting valid windows, fixed-size windows, opposite ends of a sorted array, merging two sorted lists. |
| [1.4 Binary search](1-4-binary-search/) | The invariant template, searching a sorted sequence, searching on the answer with a monotone check, the $k$-th smallest by counting. |
| [1.5 Greedy I](1-5-greedy-1/) | Exchange arguments, earliest end first, extreme choices, matching sorted lists, left-to-right forced choices, and testing a rule against brute force. |
| [1.6 Monotonic stack and deque](1-6-monotonic-stack/) | Nearest greater or smaller elements, the span where an element is the minimum, sliding-window maxima and minima. |
| [1.7 Bits](1-7-bits/) | XOR cancellation, prefix XOR, sums counted one bit at a time, deciding from the highest bit down, and the identities between sums and bits. |

## Time

Plan on **15 to 20 hours per topic**: about 3 hours to read the lesson and redo its proofs and templates, 10 to 15 hours for the ladder of 13–20 problems, and 2 hours for the drill and the checkpoint. With the reviews spread over the following two months, Phase 1 takes roughly **110 to 140 hours**. From this phase on, also take part in one live contest a week (AtCoder ABC or Codeforces Div. 3/4) and upsolve what you could not finish; the [Contest log](../contests/) keeps track of the upsolve targets.

## The Phase 1 exam

After the seven checkpoints, take the [Phase 1 exam](exam/): 8 problems in **3 hours**, one or two from each topic, in a mixed order with the topics hidden. You pass by solving at least 60% of the problems in time (5 of 8) with no Recognition miss on a solved problem, that is, without first trying a wrong tool on a problem you then solved. After the exam, a remediation map shows for each problem the topic, the card, and the lesson section to reread.
