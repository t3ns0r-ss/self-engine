## Goal

Phase 2 adds the mathematics that contest problems take for granted: primes and divisors, arithmetic modulo a prime, counting without listing, and expected values. Each topic turns a question about a huge number of objects (all numbers up to $10^7$, all $2^n$ subsets, all orders, all random outcomes) into a formula or a short loop, and each formula comes with the condition that makes it valid: a prime modulus, independent choices, labelled groups, a sum rather than a product. By the end you should solve AtCoder Beginner Contest (ABC) D–E problems that print "modulo 998244353" without hesitation, and Codeforces problems rated about **1400–1700**, the target band of this phase.

## Topics

Work through them in order; each lesson lists what it uses from the earlier ones.

| topic | what it gives you |
|---|---|
| [2.1 Number theory](2-1-number-theory/) | The sieve of Eratosthenes, smallest prime factors, trial division, loops over multiples, extended Euclid and integer equations $ax + by = c$. |
| [2.2 Modular arithmetic](2-2-modular-arithmetic/) | Fast exponentiation, Fermat's little theorem, modular inverses, fractions modulo a prime, and reducing huge exponents. |
| [2.3 Combinatorics](2-3-combinatorics/) | Product and sum rules, binomial coefficients from factorial tables, multinomials, stars and bars, inclusion–exclusion, and counting by a bijection. |
| [2.4 Expected value](2-4-expected-value/) | Linearity of expectation, indicator variables, totals by contribution, waiting times and tail sums. |

## Time

Plan on **15 to 20 hours per topic**: about 3 to 4 hours to read the lesson and redo its proofs (this phase has more of them), 10 to 15 hours for the ladder of 12–20 problems, and 2 hours for the drill and the checkpoint. With the reviews spread over the following two months, Phase 2 takes roughly **70 to 90 hours**. Keep taking part in one live contest a week and upsolving it; from now on most contests contain at least one problem from this phase.

## The Phase 2 exam

After the four checkpoints, take the [Phase 2 exam](exam/): 6 problems in **4 hours**, one or two from each topic, in a mixed order with the topics hidden. You pass by solving at least 60% of the problems in time (4 of 6) with no Recognition miss on a solved problem, that is, without first trying a wrong tool on a problem you then solved. After the exam, a remediation map shows for each problem the topic, the card, and the lesson section to reread.
