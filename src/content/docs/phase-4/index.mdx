## Goal

Phase 4 teaches you to see a graph in a problem and to choose the right question to ask of it. A map, a grid, a set of dependencies, a network of servers, a company hierarchy and a set of one-way flights are all graphs; the work is to decide whether the question is about reaching, ordering, shortest routes, grouping, a tree, or what stays connected when something fails. The seven topics go from the plain walk to the structure that lets you answer many questions on one tree, and then to the components that remain when roads are one-way or may be lost. Each lesson proves when its method is correct and lists the conditions that break it. By the end you should solve AtCoder Beginner Contest (ABC) D–F graph problems routinely and Codeforces problems rated about **1600–2000**, the target band of this phase.

## Topics

Work through them in order; each lesson lists what it uses from the earlier ones.

| topic | what it gives you |
|---|---|
| [4.1 Graph traversal](4-1-graph-traversal/) | Searches in graphs and grids, connected components, breadth-first search for shortest routes in steps, searches from several sources at once, and states that carry extra information. |
| [4.2 Graph structure](4-2-graph-structure/) | Two-colouring and odd cycles, cycles in undirected and directed graphs, and topological order of dependencies. |
| [4.3 Shortest paths](4-3-shortest-paths/) | Dijkstra for non-negative weights, 0-1 breadth-first search, Bellman–Ford for negative weights, Floyd–Warshall for all pairs, and Dijkstra over pairs of a place and extra information. |
| [4.4 Disjoint set union and spanning trees](4-4-dsu-mst/) | Merging groups, deleting in reverse, the cut property, Kruskal's and Prim's algorithms, and the largest edge on a path. |
| [4.5 Trees](4-5-trees/) | Rooting, parents and depths, subtree sums, subtrees as ranges, the diameter, and the farthest vertex from every vertex. |
| [4.6 Lowest common ancestor](4-6-lca/) | Binary lifting, the $k$-th ancestor, the lowest common ancestor, distances in a tree, minimum or sum along a path, and counting along many paths offline. |
| [4.7 Strongly connected components, bridges, articulation points](4-7-connectivity/) | Entry times and low-link values, bridges and articulation points, pieces without bridges and one-way orientation, strongly connected components, and the graph of components. |

## Time

Plan on **15 to 20 hours per topic**: about 3 hours to read the lesson and redo its proofs and traces, 10 to 15 hours for the ladder of 12–20 problems, and 2 hours for the drill and the checkpoint. With the reviews spread over the following two months, Phase 4 takes roughly **110 to 140 hours**. Keep taking part in one live contest a week; in a typical contest at least one problem is a graph problem, and it is usually the one whose statement does not say so.

## The Phase 4 exam

After the seven checkpoints, take the [Phase 4 exam](exam/): 7 problems in **4 hours**, one from each topic, in a mixed order with the topics hidden. You pass by solving at least 60% of the problems in time (5 of 7) with no Recognition miss on a solved problem, that is, without first trying a wrong tool on a problem you then solved. After the exam, a remediation map shows for each problem the topic, the card, and the lesson section to reread.
