## lc-sum-of-all-odd-length-subarrays Sum of All Odd Length Subarrays (tier 1, pattern contribution-total, set practice)
- Budget: n ≤ 100 (O(n) possible).
- Idea: arr[i] lies in (i + 1)(n − i) subarrays; ⌈that / 2⌉ of them have odd length. Sum arr[i] times that count.
- Techniques: 2.4
- Needs this topic because: summing per element instead of per subarray.
- Tier reasoning: LeetCode Easy.

## ac-abc151_e Max-Min Sums (tier 4, pattern contribution-total, set reserved, for checkpoint)
- Budget: N ≤ 10^5.
- Idea: Sort. A_i (0-based) is the maximum of C(i, K − 1) chosen sets and the minimum of C(N − 1 − i, K − 1). Answer Σ A_i·(C(i, K−1) − C(N−1−i, K−1)) mod p (normalise negatives).
- Techniques: 2.4, 2.3, 1.1
- Needs this topic because: Σ f = Σ max − Σ min, each by contribution.
- Tier reasoning: ABC E, difficulty about 1340.

## ac-abc360_e Random Swaps of Balls (tier 4, pattern indicator-linearity, set practice)
- Budget: N < p, K ≤ 10^5.
- Idea: Track q = P(black ball at position 1). One step: q' = q·(1 − 2(N−1)/N²) + (1 − q)·(2/N²), all as modular fractions. By symmetry the other positions are equally likely, so E[x] = q + (1 − q)·(N + 2)/2 mod p.
- Techniques: 2.4, 2.2
- Needs this topic because: the answer is an expected position, a sum of probabilities times positions (linearity); the probabilities are modular fractions from 2.2. Moved here from 2.2 in M4: 2.2 does not teach expectation.
- Tier reasoning: ABC E, difficulty about 1250.

## ac-abc471_e Sum of Square of Sum (tier 4, pattern contribution-total, set reserved, for drill)

## cf-312B Archer (tier 2, pattern geometric-wait, set practice) †

## cf-621C Wet Shark and Flowers (tier 4, pattern indicator-linearity, set practice) †
- Budget: n ≤ 10^5 flowers in a circle, p prime up to 10^9: O(n).
- Idea: Each flower's count is uniform in [l, r]; a pair of neighbours pays when the product of their counts is divisible by the prime p. For one flower, P(divisible by p) = (⌊r/p⌋ − ⌊(l − 1)/p⌋) / (r − l + 1); for a pair the probability is 1 − (1 − P_i)(1 − P_{i+1}). By linearity the expected total is the sum of 2000 times these probabilities over the n neighbouring pairs. (recalled)
- Techniques: 2.4, 2.1
- Needs this topic because: splitting the expectation over the n pairs (indicators); the joint distribution of all counts is never needed.
- Tier reasoning: rating 1700, tier 4 of the Phase 2 band.

## cf-204C Little Elephant and Furik and Rubik (tier 5, pattern contribution-total, set practice) †
- Budget: n ≤ 2·10^5: pairs of positions are 4·10^10, so contributions must be summed with prefix sums per letter.
- Idea: Two strings of length n; choose a substring of each, of equal length, uniformly among all such pairs, and count the positions where the letters agree. The expected value is the total, over pairs of positions (i, j) with equal letters, of min(i, j) · (n − max(i, j) + 1), divided by the number of equal-length substring pairs Σ k² for k = 1..n. Evaluate the sum by running prefix sums of the weights for each letter. (recalled)
- Techniques: 2.4, 1.2
- Needs this topic because: the contribution of a pair of positions counted as the number of substring pairs that align them; enumerating substring pairs is impossible.
- Tier reasoning: rating 2000, top of the Phase 2 ceiling.
