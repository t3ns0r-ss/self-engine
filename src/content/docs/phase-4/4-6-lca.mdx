## 1. Why this topic exists

Here is a small problem. A company is a tree with $n = 2 \cdot 10^5$ employees; employee 1 is the boss and every other employee has one direct boss. You are given $q = 2 \cdot 10^5$ questions: "who is the lowest boss that both $a$ and $b$ report to (directly or through others)?", and "how many reporting lines are between $a$ and $b$?"

The parents of [topic 4.5](../4-5-trees/) let you climb from a vertex to the root, one step at a time. To answer one question, climb the deeper vertex until both are at the same depth, then climb both together until they meet. A chain of $2 \cdot 10^5$ employees makes each climb $2 \cdot 10^5$ steps, and $q$ questions cost up to $4 \cdot 10^{10}$ steps.

The key idea: do not climb one step at a time. For every vertex store its ancestor $1, 2, 4, 8, \dots$ levels up, about $\log_2 n \approx 18$ of them. Each ancestor is two jumps of the previous size, so the table is built from the one before it. To go up $k$ levels, write $k$ in binary and take the jumps of the sizes that are in $k$: at most 18 jumps. To find the lowest common ancestor, first lift the deeper vertex to the depth of the other; then, from the largest jump to the smallest, jump both vertices whenever the two ancestors reached are *different*: they stop just below the meeting point, whose parent is the answer. This is **binary lifting**, and every question takes about 40 steps instead of $2 \cdot 10^5$. The distance between $a$ and $b$ is then $\text{depth}(a) + \text{depth}(b) - 2\,\text{depth}(\text{lca})$, and the same jump table can carry the minimum or the largest edge on the way up.

### What this topic does not cover
