## Goal

Phase 7 puts the earlier phases together. The first seven topics each combine two or more earlier topics into a tool of its own: a DP that runs over a tree or over a graph without cycles, a DP whose transition is cheap because a range structure answers it, a greedy that keeps a heap and can take a choice back, the numbers that decide who wins a game, a matrix that raises a recurrence to a huge power, and the construction of an object when only its existence is given. The last topic teaches no new algorithm; it trains the habit of choosing among all of the tools when the statement does not say which one it needs. Each lesson proves when its method is correct and lists the conditions that break it. By the end you should solve AtCoder Beginner Contest (ABC) F–G and AtCoder Regular Contest (ARC) B–C problems routinely and Codeforces problems rated about **2000–2400**, the target band of this phase.

## Topics

Work through them in order; each lesson opens with the two topics it combines.

| topic | what it gives you |
|---|---|
| [7.1 DP on trees, rerooting](7-1-tree-dp/) | Values of subtrees combined from the children, answers that pass through a vertex, the answer for every root in linear time, and the merge of knapsack tables in $O(n^2)$. |
| [7.2 DP on DAGs](7-2-dag-dp/) | Longest and shortest paths, counts of paths, DP after condensing the cycles, and states that form a graph without cycles. |
| [7.3 DP sped up with Fenwick or segment trees](7-3-dp-with-ds/) | Transitions that take a maximum or a sum over a range of earlier states, answered by a range structure instead of a loop. |
| [7.4 Greedy II: priority-queue greedy, regret greedy](7-4-greedy-2/) | Scheduling by deadlines with a heap, undoing a choice that turned out bad, and sweeping with a heap of what is available. |
| [7.5 Sprague–Grundy theorem](7-5-game-theory/) | Positions labelled win or lose, the xor of the heaps in Nim, and the numbers of games played in sums. |
| [7.6 Matrix exponentiation for linear recurrences](7-6-matrix-exponentiation/) | The $n$-th term of a recurrence for $n$ up to $10^{18}$, walks of exact length in a graph, extra terms in the state, and best values over a semiring. |
| [7.7 Constructive algorithms](7-7-constructive/) | Permutations, strings and grids built from a pattern, a necessary condition that is also enough, and lists of operations within a limit. |
| [7.8 Technique recognition](7-8-recognition/) | The routine for choosing a tool when the topic is not given, a recap of all Recognition Cards, and mixed review sets and a checkpoint. |

## Time

Plan on **15 to 20 hours per topic** for 7.1–7.7: about 3 hours to read the lesson and redo its proofs and traces, 10 to 15 hours for the ladder of 12–20 problems, and 2 hours for the drill and the checkpoint. Topic 7.8 needs less reading and more problem solving: about **15 hours**, mostly on the mixed sets. With the reviews spread over the following two months, Phase 7 takes roughly **120 to 160 hours**. Keep taking part in one live contest a week, and in each contest write the three lines of the routine (budget, candidate tools, property checked) for every problem you attempt.

## The Phase 7 exam

After the eight checkpoints, take the [Phase 7 exam](exam/): 8 problems in **4 hours**, in a mixed order with the topics hidden. You pass by solving at least 60% of the problems in time (5 of 8) with no Recognition miss on a solved problem, that is, without first trying a wrong tool on a problem you then solved. After the exam, a remediation map shows for each problem the topic, the card, and the lesson section to reread.
