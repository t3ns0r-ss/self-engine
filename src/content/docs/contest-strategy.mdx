title: Contest strategy
description: Concrete rules for the two hours of a live contest, each with its reason.
---

This page is about the clock, not about techniques. It applies to a live contest: AtCoder Beginner Contest, or a Codeforces Div. 3 or Div. 4 round (the weekly rhythm is in [Method, section 5](../method/#5-weekly-rhythm-about-15-hours)). To record a contest and its upsolve targets, use the [Contest log](../contests/); this page does not repeat it.

Every rule has one reason. If a rule's reason does not apply to you, change the rule, but write down why.

## Before the contest starts

1. **Keep one template file and never edit it during the round.** It holds the includes, fast input and output and nothing else. *Reason: a template you rewrite under time pressure is the first place a typo appears.*
2. **Check your login, your language setting and the submit page before the start.** *Reason: a login problem at minute 0 costs more than any hard problem.*

## During the contest

3. **Read every problem statement once in the first 10 minutes, and write the budget (the largest bound and the time it allows) next to each.** Do not code yet. *Reason: the order of difficulty on the scoreboard is a guess, and you find a problem you can solve quickly only by reading them all.*
4. **Solve in this order: A, B, then the problem with the most accepted solutions so far.** Check the standings for the count once, after you have read everything. *Reason: points go to solved problems, and the solved count is the best free estimate of how hard a problem is for people at your level.*
5. **Give each problem a time box before you start it: about 10 minutes for A, 20 for B, 30 for C.** When the box ends, write down in one line where you are stuck, then decide to skip. *Reason: without a limit, one stuck problem eats the time of three you could solve.*
6. **Skip a problem when you have no idea of the technique, or when your idea fails on a sample or on a hand-made small case.** Do not skip because the code is long. *Reason: a missing idea does not appear by waiting, but long code is only time.*
7. **Come back to a skipped problem only after every problem you can solve is submitted.** *Reason: a submitted problem is points you have, and a skipped one is only a chance of them.*
8. **Before every submission, re-read the constraints and the output format, then run the samples and one case you made up (smallest input, all values equal).** *Reason: the common wrong answers are overflow, a missing edge case and a wrong output line, and all three show up in the constraints or in a tiny input.*
9. **After a wrong answer, do not resubmit a changed guess.** Make a small random test and a slow brute force first if the problem allows it, or re-read the statement for the line you skipped. *Reason: every wrong submission costs penalty time, and a guess that happens to pass hides the bug until the next contest.*
10. **In the last 15 minutes, only fix or submit things you already have.** Do not start a problem whose idea you do not yet have. *Reason: a half-finished solution scores nothing, and a late wrong submission only adds penalty.*

## After the contest

11. **The same day: write down, for each problem you did not solve, the card or idea it needed (or "unknown") and the mistake category from the [Method](../method/#4-mistake-categories).** *Reason: the log is how recognition misses become visible.*
12. **Upsolve within 2 days: solve each missed problem rated up to 300 points above your rating, using hints in the usual order.** *Reason: the idea is fresh and the editorial reads as an answer to a question you already asked.*
13. **Re-solve after 7 days any problem for which you needed hint 2, hint 3 or the editorial, without looking at your old code.** The home page lists it for you. *Reason: understanding the editorial is not the same as being able to find the idea alone, and only the second one wins contests.*
14. **Upsolve is for the problems you could not solve in the contest. Re-solve is for the problems you solved with help.** Do not count a re-solve as an upsolve. *Reason: the first measures what you could not do, the second whether you can now; mixing them hides which one is the problem.*

## Practice: the speed side

Contest speed is mostly implementation speed. The [Implementation drills](../drills/) are 3 to 5 minute exercises for the bugs that cost the most minutes, and each one is listed in the earliest lesson that has all it needs.
