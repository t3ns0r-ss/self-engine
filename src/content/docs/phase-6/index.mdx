## Goal

Phase 6 teaches you to see the structure inside a string and to choose the tool that makes a question about many substrings cheap. A question about a text can ask whether two pieces are equal, where a pattern occurs, how far a word reaches into a dictionary, or how all the suffixes stand in dictionary order; each of these has its own tool, and none of them is the plain comparison of letters, which costs too much when the strings have $10^5$ or $10^6$ letters. The four topics go from a number that stands for a piece of text, to arrays that describe how a string agrees with itself, to a tree that shares the beginnings of many words, and then to the sorted list of all suffixes with the common beginnings of neighbours. Each lesson proves when its method is correct and lists the conditions that break it. By the end you should solve AtCoder Beginner Contest (ABC) E–F string problems routinely and Codeforces problems rated about **1900–2200**, the target band of this phase.

## Topics

Work through them in order; each lesson lists what it uses from the earlier ones.

| topic | what it gives you |
|---|---|
| [6.1 Polynomial hashing](6-1-string-hashing/) | Equality of any two blocks of a string in one step, counting and grouping equal strings, binary search on the length of a repeated block, and palindrome tests in one step, with a random base so that no test can be aimed at the program. |
| [6.2 Prefix function and Z-function](6-2-prefix-function/) | All occurrences of a pattern in a text, the borders and periods of a string and its repetitions, and how far every suffix agrees with the beginning, with the proofs that make the linear time honest. |
| [6.3 Trie and binary trie](6-3-trie/) | Counts and best values for the beginnings of many strings, a walk of the text down a tree of words, and the largest XOR or the count of XORs below a bound with a tree of binary digits. |
| [6.4 Suffix array and LCP array](6-4-suffix-array/) | The sorted suffixes of a string in $O(n \log n)$ and the common beginnings of neighbours: counting and finding different substrings, many patterns in one text, the smallest rotation, and common substrings of several strings. |

## Time

Plan on **15 to 20 hours per topic**: about 3 hours to read the lesson and redo its proofs and traces, 10 to 15 hours for the ladder of 12–20 problems, and 2 hours for the drill and the checkpoint. With the reviews spread over the following two months, Phase 6 takes roughly **70 to 90 hours**. Keep taking part in one live contest a week; in a typical contest a string problem is a question about equal pieces, beginnings or occurrences, and the work is to notice which one.

## The Phase 6 exam

After the four checkpoints, take the [Phase 6 exam](exam/): 6 problems in **4 hours**, in a mixed order with the topics hidden. You pass by solving at least 60% of the problems in time (4 of 6) with no Recognition miss on a solved problem, that is, without first trying a wrong tool on a problem you then solved. After the exam, a remediation map shows for each problem the topic, the card, and the lesson section to reread.
