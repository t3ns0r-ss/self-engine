## Goal

Phase 3 turns "try every possibility" into "remember what matters". It starts with recursion and backtracking, which list every choice, and then shows the step that makes most contest problems fast: notice that the rest of the problem depends only on a small **state**, and compute each state once. The eight topics are the shapes that state takes in practice: a position, a capacity, a pair of prefixes, a cell of a grid, a segment, a subset of a small set, a digit position with a tight flag. Each lesson proves when its table is correct and lists the conditions that break it. By the end you should solve AtCoder Beginner Contest (ABC) D–E dynamic programming problems routinely and Codeforces problems rated about **1500–1900**, the target band of this phase.

## Topics

Work through them in order; each lesson lists what it uses from the earlier ones.

| topic | what it gives you |
|---|---|
| [3.1 Backtracking](3-1-backtracking/) | Recursion over choices, generating all subsets and orders, placing items under constraints, pruning with bounds, and meet in the middle. |
| [3.2 DP foundations](3-2-dp-foundations/) | States and transitions, counting by the last choice, tabulation and memoisation, small facts added to the state, and rebuilding an optimal solution. |
| [3.3 Knapsack](3-3-knapsack/) | 0/1 and unbounded knapsack, indexing by value, counting combinations against sequences, reachable subset sums with bitsets, and limited copies. |
| [3.4 Sequence DP](3-4-sequence-dp/) | The longest increasing subsequence in $O(n \log n)$, reductions to it after sorting, the longest common subsequence, and edit distance. |
| [3.5 Counting DP](3-5-counting-dp/) | Paths in grids, sequences with a small state, sums of allowed parts, and transitions over a range with prefix sums, all modulo a prime. |
| [3.6 Interval DP](3-6-interval-dp/) | Tables over segments: the best split point, games taking from the ends, palindromes inside segments, and pairing the first element with a partner. |
| [3.7 Bitmask DP](3-7-bitmask-dp/) | Assignments over the set of used items, paths through every vertex, packing into the fewest groups, and iterating over submasks. |
| [3.8 Digit DP](3-8-digit-dp/) | Counting numbers up to $N$ with a digit property: the tight walk, digit sums and remainders, leading zeros, and totals over a range. |

## Time

Plan on **15 to 20 hours per topic**: about 3 hours to read the lesson and redo its proofs and traces, 10 to 15 hours for the ladder of 12–20 problems, and 2 hours for the drill and the checkpoint. With the reviews spread over the following two months, Phase 3 takes roughly **130 to 160 hours**, the longest phase so far. Keep taking part in one live contest a week; from now on most ABC D and E problems and many Codeforces Div. 2 C and D problems use one of these tables.

## The Phase 3 exam

After the eight checkpoints, take the [Phase 3 exam](exam/): 8 problems in **4 hours**, one from each topic, in a mixed order with the topics hidden. You pass by solving at least 60% of the problems in time (5 of 8) with no Recognition miss on a solved problem, that is, without first trying a wrong tool on a problem you then solved. After the exam, a remediation map shows for each problem the topic, the card, and the lesson section to reread.
