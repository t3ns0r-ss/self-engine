## lc-guess-number-higher-or-lower Guess Number Higher or Lower (tier 2, pattern halving, set practice)
- Budget: n up to 2^31 - 1; about 31 calls to guess().
- Idea: The judge function guess(m) says whether the picked number is lower than, higher than or equal to m. Binary search with lo + (hi - lo) / 2 (no overflow), at most 31 calls.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.4
- Needs this topic because: the base case of the card: a three-way reply and a plain halving.
- Tier reasoning: Easy.

## ac-abc299_d Find by Query (tier 3, pattern halving, set practice)
- Budget: N up to 2·10^5, at most 20 questions: ceil(log2(N - 1)) = 18.
- Idea: S_1 = 0 and S_N = 1. Keep S_lo = 0 and S_hi = 1; ask the middle; when hi = lo + 1 print lo. Flush after every question, and quit right after the answer.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.4
- Needs this topic because: worked example 1: the invariant form of Theorem 1.4.1 with questions instead of array reads.
- Tier reasoning: ABC D.

## cf-1807E Interview (tier 3, pattern halving, set practice) †
- Budget: (recalled) n up to 2·10^5, at most 30 questions: 18 are enough.
- Idea: (recalled) n piles of stones, all stones weigh 1 gram except those of one pile, which weigh 2 grams; a question gives the total weight of the stones in a chosen set of piles. With prefix sums of the pile sizes, the reply for piles 1..m is larger than the prefix sum exactly when the heavy pile is among them: binary search on m.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.4, 1.2
- Needs this topic because: a monotone reply built from a prefix sum (1.2).
- Tier reasoning: Rating 1300.

## cf-1520F1 Guess the K-th Zero (Easy version) (tier 5, pattern halving, set practice) †
- Budget: (recalled) n up to 2·10^5, at most 25 questions: 18 are enough.
- Idea: (recalled) a question gives the sum of a range of a 0/1 array. The number of zeros among the first m cells is m minus the reply for [1, m], and it never decreases with m: find the smallest m where it reaches k.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.4, 1.2
- Needs this topic because: the reply is a count, and the predicate "at least k zeros so far" is monotone.
- Tier reasoning: Rating 1600 is tier 5 (the boundary goes to the higher tier).

## cf-1103B Game with modulo (tier 5, pattern halving, set practice) †
- Budget: (recalled) the hidden number a is up to 10^9 and at most 60 questions are allowed: about 30 + 30.
- Idea: (recalled) the upper end is not known, so double a probe until the reply shows that it passed a, then halve the range between the last two probes. Doubling and halving each cost about log2 of a.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.4
- Needs this topic because: search without a given upper bound: doubling first, then the same invariant.
- Tier reasoning: Rating 2000.

## cf-1479A Searching Local Minimum (tier 5, pattern compare-halving, set practice) †
- Budget: (recalled) n up to 10^5, at most 100 questions: 2 per round and 17 rounds is 34.
- Idea: (recalled) the numbers form a permutation and the outside counts as infinite. Keep a segment whose left neighbour is larger and whose right neighbour is smaller; ask two neighbouring numbers in the middle and move to the side where the numbers go down.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.4
- Needs this topic because: Theorem 1.8.2: a comparison of two neighbours decides the side.
- Tier reasoning: Rating 1700.

## cf-1486C2 Guessing the Greatest (hard version) (tier 5, pattern compare-halving, set practice) †
- Budget: (recalled) n up to 10^5 and at most 20 questions: one question per halving step, 17 steps.
- Idea: (recalled) a question gives the position of the second largest number of a range. The largest is on the same side of that position as the answer says, so keep a range that contains the largest and reuse the previous reply: ask again on one side, and the reply repeats the same position exactly when the largest is inside that side.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.4
- Needs this topic because: a comparison reply that points to the side; the budget forces one question per step.
- Tier reasoning: Rating 1900.

## cf-1019B The hat (tier 5, pattern compare-halving, set practice) †
- Budget: (recalled) n up to 10^5 and at most 60 questions: 2 per round and 16 rounds.
- Idea: (recalled) n students sit in a circle with neighbouring numbers differing by exactly 1. Look at the difference of student i and the student opposite: it has opposite signs at i and at the opposite position, and it changes by at most 2 between neighbours, so it passes through 0. Binary search on the sign of the difference; if n mod 4 = 2 the answer is -1.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.4
- Needs this topic because: the invariant is a sign change, as in the local minimum search.
- Tier reasoning: Rating 2000.

## ac-abc337_e Bad Juice (tier 4, pattern equation-queries, set practice)
- Budget: N up to 100: m = ceil(log2 N) = 7 friends; all lines are printed before the replies.
- Idea: Friend j drinks the bottles whose number minus 1 has bit j set; the sick friends spell the number of the bad bottle. The number of friends must be the fewest, so Theorem 1.8.1 gives m. The judge may change the bad bottle while consistent: the design must make every pattern single.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.7
- Needs this topic because: a non-adaptive design: every reply is one bit of the answer.
- Tier reasoning: ABC E.

## cf-1425F Flamingoes of Mystery (tier 4, pattern equation-queries, set practice) †
- Budget: (recalled) n up to 10^3 and at most n questions.
- Idea: (recalled) a question gives the sum of the numbers in a range of at least 2 cages. Ask the sum of cages 1 to i for every i from 2 to n (n - 1 questions: prefix sums from the second one), and the sum of cages 2 and 3 as the last question. Then a_1 = P_3 - (a_2 + a_3), and the other numbers are differences of prefix sums.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.2
- Needs this topic because: the replies are sums of ranges: prefix sums (1.2) turned back into numbers.
- Tier reasoning: Rating 1400 is tier 4 (the boundary goes to the higher tier).

## cf-1556D Take a Guess (tier 5, pattern equation-queries, set practice) †
- Budget: (recalled) n up to 10^4, at most 2n questions of the kinds "and i j" and "or i j".
- Idea: (recalled) a + b = (a or b) + (a and b), so two questions per pair give pair sums. As in Theorem 1.8.3, three pairs give a_1, a_2, a_3, then one pair per further number. Sort the numbers and print the k-th smallest.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.7, 1.1
- Needs this topic because: bit identity (1.7) turns two replies into one sum, then the sum equations of the card.
- Tier reasoning: Rating 1800.

## cf-1451E1 Bitwise Queries (Easy Version) (tier 5, pattern equation-queries, set practice) †
- Budget: (recalled) n a power of two up to 2^16 and at most n + 1 questions of the kinds and, or, xor.
- Idea: (recalled) xor each number with a_1 (n - 1 questions). The values lie in 0..n-1, so either two of them are equal, and one "and" question on that pair gives its value, or they are all different and the values 0 and n-1 identify two numbers whose "and" questions give a_1.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.7
- Needs this topic because: xor replies chain every number to a_1; a pigeonhole argument finds the missing anchor.
- Tier reasoning: Rating 2000.

## cf-1503B 3-Coloring (tier 5, pattern any-reply, set practice) †
- Budget: (recalled) n up to 100, an n by n board; each round the judge names a colour that you must not use.
- Idea: (recalled) colour the board like a chessboard: the cells of one parity get the colour 1 and the cells of the other parity the colour 2; when the judge forbids one of them, place the other on a cell of the matching parity, and use the colour 3 when that parity is full. No two neighbours then share a colour whatever the judge names.
- Techniques: 1.8
- Needs this topic because: a pairing-like invariant that survives every request.
- Tier reasoning: Rating 1700.

## ac-abc313_d Odd or Even (tier 3, pattern equation-queries, set reserved, for checkpoint)
- Budget: N up to 1000, at most N questions, K odd; the judge is adaptive.
- Idea: Each question gives the parity of the sum of K chosen values. Ask the K + 1 sets that leave out one of the first K + 1 positions: the xor of the K + 1 replies is the parity S of all K + 1 values, and value j is S xor reply j. Then one question per further position j, with the K - 1 known values 1 .. K - 1 and j: N questions in all.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.7
- Needs this topic because: the replies are xor equations; the adaptive judge forces a single consistent answer (Theorem 1.8.4).
- Tier reasoning: ABC D.

## ac-abc355_e Guess the Sum (tier 4, pattern equation-queries, set reserved, for drill)
- Budget: N up to 18, the number of questions must equal the least possible.
- Idea: The allowed questions are the blocks of length 2^i that start at a multiple of 2^i. The range L..R is cut into the fewest such blocks by taking from the left the largest block that fits (the bits of the position tell its size), then the answer is the sum of the replies.
- Techniques: 1.8, 1.7, 1.5
- Needs this topic because: blocks given by bits (1.7) and a greedy choice (1.5).
- Tier reasoning: ABC E.
