## 1. Why the programme is ordered this way

**Strict dependencies.** Each topic uses only itself and topics before it. When a lesson needs an idea, that idea has already been taught and proved. You never meet a technique by name before its lesson.

**Pure ladders.** Every problem in a topic's ladder can be solved with that topic and earlier ones, and needs the topic's technique to fit the time limit. Each rung adds exactly one new twist over the rung before it, so you always know what the problem is meant to teach.

**Mixing comes late, and on purpose.** Inside a topic you know which technique is being trained, so the ladder trains *using* the technique. Contests never tell you the topic, so recognition is trained separately: by identification drills (which include earlier topics), by phase exams with the topics hidden, and by the whole of Phase 7.

## 2. How to recognise which technique a problem needs

Read every problem in three layers.

1. **Constraints → budget.** A judge runs about $10^8$ simple operations per second. The largest bound fixes the growth rate you can afford. For example, $n \le 2 \cdot 10^5$ allows $O(n \log n)$ but not $O(n^2)$, since $(2 \cdot 10^5)^2 = 4 \cdot 10^{10}$ operations take about 400 seconds.
2. **Question form → candidate tools.** Phrases like "longest contiguous segment" or "count pairs" are *weak signals*. They suggest a few candidate tools but prove nothing: many different problems share the same wording.
3. **Structural property → decision.** Each technique works because of one property, the precondition of its correctness proof. Lessons call it the *decisive property*. Check it on the actual problem, with the actual constraints (for example: "can values be negative?"). The property, not the wording, decides.

Each lesson gives you Recognition Cards: the decisive property, how to test it, the weak signals, the *kill signals* that rule the card out, and the look-alike problems that need something else.

## 3. Routine for every problem

1. Before coding, write three lines in the log panel: the **budget**, the **candidate tools**, and the **property you verified**.
2. Struggle before asking for help. Work for at least **20 minutes** before Hint 1 in Phases 0–1, and **30–45 minutes** from Phase 2 on. Wait at least **10 minutes** between hints. The site reminds you before showing each hint.
3. Submit on the platform itself (Codeforces, AtCoder, CSES, LeetCode). This site does not judge code.
4. Log the outcome: status, minutes spent, and the main mistake category if something went wrong.

If you needed hint 2 or 3, or read the editorial, the problem comes back as a **re-solve** seven days later on the home page. It stays there until you solve it without hints.

## 4. Mistake categories

| Category | Example |
|---|---|
| Recognition miss | You used a shrinking window for "longest subarray with sum at most K", but values could be negative, so the window method did not apply. |
| Property error | You picked the right tool but relied on a property that does not hold here, for example that the answer is monotone when it is not. |
| Implementation bug | An off-by-one in a loop bound, or a variable not reset between test cases. |
| Edge case | The program fails for n = 1, for all values equal, or for the largest values. |
| Complexity misjudgement | You wrote an $O(n^2)$ solution for $n = 2 \cdot 10^5$, or missed that an inner loop is not amortised. |

Recognition misses matter most: the home page shows which cards you miss most often.

## 5. Weekly rhythm (about 15 hours)

- **4 days:** the current topic. Lesson, worked examples, then ladder problems in order.
- **1 day:** due review sets and re-solves.
- **1 day:** a live contest (AtCoder Beginner Contest, or Codeforces Div. 3 or Div. 4), from Phase 1 on, followed by upsolving.
- **1 day:** rest.

## 6. Upsolving

After every contest, solve every problem you missed that is rated up to 300 points above your current rating. Record them in the [contest log](/contests/) and tick them off there.

## 7. When to move on

**Topic.** Move to the next topic when you have passed its checkpoint: at least 2 of its 3 problems solved within the time limit, and at least 8 drill items identified correctly. Finishing most of the ladder first is the best preparation. Review sets for the topic become due 3, 14, and 60 days after you pass.

**Phase.** After the last topic of a phase, take the phase exam: 6–10 unseen problems with the topics hidden. You pass with at least 60% solved in time and no recognition misses on the problems you solved. If you do not pass, use the exam's remediation map: reread the lesson sections it names, redo those topics' review sets, and upsolve the exam problems before starting the next phase.

## 8. Sending feedback

When something is confusing, wrong, too hard, or too easy, write it in `FEEDBACK.md` in the repository. The [Progress](/progress/) page has a **Copy feedback** button that turns your logged mistakes into notes in the right format, grouped by topic and card. Every open item is handled before new lessons are written.
